# Criterion C5 — Nomological Validity

| | |
|---|---|
| Validity type | Construct |
| Pass condition | The claim fits into a broader network of established findings and theoretical expectations |
| Evidence family | Cross-cutting |
| Minimum reporting | Named theoretical framework, specific predictions it makes about this mechanism, whether they hold |
| Common failure mode | Treating a standalone finding as complete without connecting it to related work |

## What this criterion requires

Nomological validity asks whether the claimed mechanism fits into a broader theory — whether its properties are consistent with what would be expected given related findings and established principles.

Satisfied when:

1. **The mechanism is placed in a theoretical network.** The claim states what broader framework the mechanism belongs to and what that framework predicts about it.
2. **Predictions from the network are tested.** If the theoretical framework predicts that mechanism X should have property Y, Y is measured.
3. **Anomalies are acknowledged.** If the mechanism violates a prediction from the broader theory, the violation is stated and explained.

## MI example

Induction heads are placed in a broader theory of in-context learning: Olsson et al. argue that induction heads are the mechanism for general in-context learning. The nomological network predicts that removing induction heads should degrade all in-context learning, not just copying. This prediction is tested and fails — induction heads are necessary for token copying but not for general ICL — which is why the general ICL claim is Disconfirmed while the token-copying claim reaches Triangulated.

## Connection to the chain

Required for Validated tier. Without nomological validity, the mechanism is characterized in isolation. Nomological validity connects it to a larger body of knowledge.
